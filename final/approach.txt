1. PROBLEM UNDERSTANDING
================================================================================

The task is to predict normalized traffic demand scores (in [0, 1]) for specific
geohash locations at specific 15-minute timestamps on Day 49, given historical
traffic data from Days 48–49 along with road infrastructure and weather features.

Key challenges:
  - Zero-inflated distribution: Many geohashes have near-zero demand values.
  - Spatial autocorrelation: Neighboring geohashes share traffic patterns.
  - Temporal periodicity: Traffic follows diurnal cycles (morning/evening peaks).
  - Mixed feature types: Geohash strings, road type categories, lane counts,
    and weather conditions all contribute to demand.
  - Missing values: RoadType is missing for a subset of rows.

================================================================================
2. DATA OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Training Data (train.csv):
  - 77,299 rows across Days 48–49
  - Columns: Index, geohash, day, timestamp, demand, RoadType, NumberofLanes,
             LargeVehicles, Landmarks, Temperature, Weather

Test Data (test.csv):
  - 41,778 rows on Day 49
  - Same columns as train but without the 'demand' target

Key statistics:
  - 1,249 unique geohash locations (6-character precision, ~1.2km × 0.6km cells)
  - Timestamps in 15-minute intervals from 00:00 to 23:45 (96 slots/day)
  - Demand range: [0, 1], highly right-skewed with many near-zero values
  - Road types: Residential, Street, Highway

Empirically observed road-type demand bounds (used as physical constraints):
  - Residential : [0.000000, 0.219997]
  - Street      : [0.220016, 0.349908]
  - Highway     : [0.350009, 1.000000]

================================================================================
3. PRE-PROCESSING
================================================================================

3.1 HIERARCHICAL ROAD TYPE IMPUTATION
--------------------------------------
Missing RoadType values are imputed in descending specificity order:

  Priority 1: Exact geohash + NumberofLanes + LargeVehicles + Landmarks match
  Priority 2: geohash + NumberofLanes + LargeVehicles
  Priority 3: geohash + NumberofLanes
  Priority 4: NumberofLanes + LargeVehicles + Landmarks
  Priority 5: NumberofLanes + LargeVehicles
  Priority 6: geohash only
  
  Rule fallbacks:
  - NumberofLanes ≥ 4 → Highway
  - Lanes == 1 & LargeVehicles == "Not Allowed" & Landmarks == "Yes" → Street
  - Remaining → Residential

================================================================================
4. FEATURE ENGINEERING
================================================================================

4.1 TEMPORAL FEATURES (Cyclical Manifold Projection)
------------------------------------------------------
Raw timestamps (HH:MM) are converted to integer minutes, then projected onto
2D unit circles to preserve temporal continuity across midnight:

  slot       = minute // 15           (0–95, 96 slots per day)
  hour       = minute // 60           (0–23)
  time_sin   = sin(2π × slot / 96)
  time_cos   = cos(2π × slot / 96)
  hour_sin   = sin(2π × hour / 24)
  hour_cos   = cos(2π × hour / 24)

This projection is critical: direct integer encoding creates discontinuities
(23→0) that gradient-boosting models cannot bridge.

4.2 SPATIAL FEATURES (Geohash Topology & K-Means Clustering)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Geohash strings encode Z-order space-filling curves, meaning nearby cells
may have numerically different hash values. We address this via:

  a) Prefix Hierarchy Decomposition:
     - gh2: First 2 characters (~1250km precision)
     - gh3: First 3 characters (~156km precision)
     - gh4: First 4 characters (~20km precision)
     - gh5: First 5 characters (~2.4km precision)

  b) K-Means Geospatial Clustering (50 clusters):
     Each geohash is decoded to WGS-84 lat/lon using Z-order de-interleaving,
     then K-Means (k=50) groups spatially adjacent cells. The cluster label
     (geo_cluster) completely bypasses Z-curve discontinuities.

  c) WGS-84 Coordinates (lat, lon) directly included as numeric features.

4.3 DAY-48 EXACT LAGS & SHIFTED LAGS
--------------------------------------
  lag_d48_exact  : Day-48 demand at same geohash × minute
  d48_prev15     : Day-48 demand at t − 15 min
  d48_next15     : Day-48 demand at t + 15 min
  d48_prev30     : Day-48 demand at t − 30 min
  d48_prev60     : Day-48 demand at t − 60 min

4.4 MULTI-RESOLUTION SPATIAL LAGS (Research: +0.5–1% R²)
----------------------------------------------------------
  gh5_time_mean  : Mean Day-48 demand at same gh5 prefix × minute
  gh4_time_mean  : Mean Day-48 demand at same gh4 prefix × minute
  gh3_time_mean  : Mean Day-48 demand at same gh3 prefix × minute
  cluster_time_mean: Mean Day-48 demand at same geo_cluster × minute

4.5 DAY-OVER-DAY (DoD) RATIO FEATURES — #1 MISSING FEATURE FROM GRAB AI!
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per-geohash demand ratio between Day 49 (known slots) and Day 48:

  global_dod     = sum(D49_demand) / sum(D48_demand)      [global scaling]
  dod_ratio_geo  = per-geohash D49/D48 ratio (Laplace-smoothed)
  dod_ratio_gh5  = per-gh5-prefix D49/D48 ratio
  dod_ratio_road = per-RoadType D49/D48 ratio

  dod_blend = 0.55 × dod_ratio_geo
            + 0.25 × dod_ratio_gh5
            + 0.12 × dod_ratio_road
            + 0.08 × dod_global

  prior_dod = lag_d48_exact × dod_blend   ← Key prediction anchor

4.6 DAY-49 SLOPE / TRAJECTORY FEATURES (Research: Most impactful!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Per-geohash linear regression fit over known Day-49 demand slots:

  geo_slope49       : Demand trend slope (change per minute)
  geo_intercept49   : Demand at minute = 0
  geo_cv49          : Coefficient of variation (demand variability)
  slope_extrapolated = geo_intercept49 + geo_slope49 × minute

4.7 DAY-49 SHORT LAGS & ROLLING WINDOWS
-----------------------------------------
  lag49_t15   : Known Day-49 demand at t − 15 min
  lag49_t30   : Known Day-49 demand at t − 30 min
  lag49_t60   : Known Day-49 demand at t − 60 min
  roll_1h     : 1-hour rolling mean of Day-49 known demand
  roll_2h     : 2-hour rolling mean of Day-49 known demand

4.8 SLOT-TO-HOUR RATIO (Research: +0.3–0.8% R²)
-------------------------------------------------
  geo_hour_mean       : Mean Day-48 demand per geohash × hour
  slot_to_hour_ratio  = lag_d48_exact / geo_hour_mean
                        (how much this slot deviates from hourly average)

4.9 ROAD-TYPE PERCENTILE RANK (Research: +0.2–0.5% R²)
-------------------------------------------------------
  geo_rank_in_road : Percentile rank of this geohash's demand within its
                     RoadType × minute group (based on Day-48 data)

4.10 WEATHER & PEAK-HOUR INTERACTION FEATURES
----------------------------------------------
  weather_sev     : Ordinal weather severity (Sunny=0, Foggy=1, Rainy=2, Snowy=3)
  is_adverse      : Binary (weather_sev ≥ 2)
  is_morning_peak : Hours 7–9
  is_evening_peak : Hours 17–20
  weather_x_peak  : weather_sev × is_peak
  rain_x_morning  : is_rain × is_morning_peak
  temp_x_peak     : temp × is_peak
  temp_x_lanes    : temp × NumberofLanes
  adverse_x_lanes : is_adverse × NumberofLanes

4.11 INFRASTRUCTURE INTERACTION FEATURES
-----------------------------------------
  is_large_allowed : LargeVehicles == "Allowed"
  has_landmark     : Landmarks == "Yes"
  lanes_x_large    : NumberofLanes × is_large_allowed
  lanes_x_landmark : NumberofLanes × has_landmark

5. MODEL ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

We train an ensemble of 6 diverse learners, then fuse them via a Ridge
meta-learner learned from chronological out-of-fold (OOF) predictions.

5.1 BASE LEARNERS
-----------------

  Model 1 — LightGBM Tweedie (p=1.2):
    Tweedie distribution handles zero-inflated, right-skewed demand natively.
    Hyperparameters: n_estimators=400, lr=0.04, num_leaves=63, subsample=0.85,
                     colsample=0.82, reg_alpha=0.2, reg_lambda=6.0

  Model 2 — LightGBM Huber (alpha=0.9):
    Robust to outliers in high-demand highway segments.
    Hyperparameters: n_estimators=350, lr=0.04, num_leaves=63

  Model 3 — LightGBM DART (Tweedie p=1.3):
    Dropout Additive Regression Trees — prevents overfitting via random
    tree dropout during prediction. Adds prediction diversity.
    Hyperparameters: n_estimators=300, drop_rate=0.1

  Model 4 — XGBoost Tweedie (p=1.2):
    Alternative GBM implementation for ensemble diversity.
    Hyperparameters: n_estimators=300, lr=0.04, max_depth=7,
                     min_child_weight=15, subsample=0.85, colsample=0.82

  Model 5 — ExtraTrees:
    Extremely Randomized Trees — full-depth growth (min_samples_leaf=1) to
    capture fine-grained geohash-specific demand patterns.
    Categorical features: OrdinalEncoder preprocessing.
    Hyperparameters: n_estimators=300, max_features=0.72

  Model 6 — CatBoost (if available):
    Native categorical handling via ordered boosting without manual encoding.
    Hyperparameters: iterations=800, lr=0.04, depth=8, l2_leaf_reg=5,
                     bagging_temperature=0.8, rsm=0.8, boosting_type=Ordered

5.2 RIDGE META-LEARNER (alpha=5)
---------------------------------
  - 3-fold chronological OOF validation on Day-49 (cutoffs: 90, 105, 150 min)
  - Ridge regression (positive=True) fits optimal blend weights
  - Grid search over alpha ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20}
  - ExtraTrees guardrail: if ExtraTrees alone beats the blend, use it exclusively

5.3 PER-GEOHASH RESIDUAL BIAS CORRECTION
------------------------------------------
  After final training, compute per-geohash systematic errors on known Day-49 slots:
  
    bias_geo = mean(D49_actual − model_prediction) per geohash

  Apply correction to test predictions, capped at ±0.03 to prevent over-correction:
  
    final_pred = clip(blend_pred + clip(bias_geo, -0.03, 0.03), 0, 1)

5.4 ROAD-TYPE PHYSICAL BOUNDS (POST-PROCESSING)
------------------------------------------------
  Apply empirically observed demand bounds per road type:
    Residential → clip(pred, 0.0,      0.219997)
    Street      → clip(pred, 0.220016, 0.349908)
    Highway     → clip(pred, 0.350009, 1.0)

================================================================================
6. VALIDATION STRATEGY
================================================================================

Due to the temporal nature of the dataset (train=Days 48–49, test=Day 49):

  - Chronological OOF: 3 folds using Day-49 chronological cutoffs
    Fold 1: Train on Day48 + D49[0:90min],   Validate on D49[90min:]
    Fold 2: Train on Day48 + D49[0:105min],  Validate on D49[105min:]
    Fold 3: Train on Day48 + D49[0:150min],  Validate on D49[150min:]

  - This ensures no temporal leakage in feature construction

================================================================================
10. KEY DESIGN DECISIONS
================================================================================

  1. Cyclical time encoding prevents midnight boundary artifacts.
  2. K-Means on decoded lat/lon avoids Z-curve spatial discontinuities.
  3. Geohash prefix hierarchy captures multi-scale demand patterns.
  4. Tweedie distribution handles zero-inflated demand distribution.
  5. DoD ratio is the single most impactful feature (identified in Grab AI research).
  6. Day-49 slope extrapolation anchors predictions using observed trajectory.
  7. Ridge meta-learner with ExtraTrees guardrail prevents over-blending.
  8. Per-geohash bias correction fixes systematic errors on known D49 slots.
  9. Road-type physical bounds enforce domain constraints post-prediction.
